Sound, music and brain entrainment

This scorecard presents Universal Mind’s assessment of research informing the wider Universal Mind project. Our initial clinical focus is anxiety and PTSD, alongside research into music, brain entrainment and physiological responses.

The B grade reflects this wider evidence. Research into gene expression is discussed separately below.

Why B?

Recent evidence supports qualified music interventions for anxiety and PTSD symptoms. A 2024 comparative EEG study provides a direct signal for isochronic-derived stimulation, while theta-frequency binaural-beat research supports focused testing within the 5-8 Hz range. A small 528 Hz study reports short-term endocrine and mood changes that warrant replication.

The grade applies to the research described here. It does not establish the effectiveness of every sound intervention or a particular Universal Mind experience.

Key studies

  1. 2025 · eClinicalMedicine

    Music therapy for the treatment of anxiety: a systematic review with multilevel meta-analyses

    Systematic review and meta-analysis · 51 studies

    Fifty-one studies and 3,276 participants produced a medium overall effect on anxiety, including a medium effect for self-reported anxiety.

    Why it matters: A substantial synthesis supporting music therapy for anxiety.

    Limit: Physiological outcomes were small and non-significant; 39.2% of included studies were rated weak.

  2. 2024 · Journal of Mood & Anxiety Disorders

    Music interventions for posttraumatic stress disorder: A systematic review

    Systematic review · 14 studies

    Fourteen studies were included; 13 reported decreased PTSD symptoms, and passive-listening studies also showed promise.

    Why it matters: Directly relevant to Universal Mind’s initial focus on PTSD.

    Limit: Nine studies were feasibility studies; small samples and weak control groups limit certainty.

  3. 2024 · Neuroscience

    Brain wave modulation and EEG power changes during auditory beats stimulation

    Comparative EEG study · 28 participants

    In 28 healthy participants, four minutes of isochronic-derived stimulation produced greater gamma-band EEG power changes than gamma binaural beats and white noise, alongside a distinct time-related alpha response.

    Why it matters: Measures how different auditory stimuli affect brain activity.

    Limit: The stimulus alternated audible frequencies rather than using a conventional fixed-carrier isochronic tone and did not test Universal Mind's exact protocol. It measured short-term EEG responses, not a clinical outcome.

  4. 2018 · Health

    Effect of 528 Hz Music on the Endocrine System and Autonomic Nervous System

    Small crossover study · 9 participants

    After five minutes of 528 Hz music, mean salivary oxytocin rose from 37.57 to 73.58 and cortisol later fell from 0.43 to 0.25; neither change was significant with 440 Hz music.

    Why it matters: Mean salivary oxytocin nearly doubled after five minutes of music.

    Limit: Only nine healthy adults participated. The short comparison is intriguing biomarker evidence, not clinical proof, and requires larger independent preregistered replication.

  5. 2026 · EXPLORE

    Efficacy of theta binaural beat therapy on pain, cognition and anxiety in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Systematic review and meta-analysis · 13 randomized trials

    Across 13 RCTs, theta (4-8 Hz) binaural beats reduced pain, showed anxiety benefit mainly in procedural and chronic-pain settings, and improved episodic memory—supporting focused testing in Universal Mind's 5-8 Hz range.

    Why it matters: Examines theta binaural beats across clinical and cognitive outcomes.

    Limit: Overall pain evidence was low certainty, outcomes varied, and the review does not establish PTSD efficacy or validate a complete sound layer.

Sound and gene expression

Research already connects music with measured gene-expression changes. The studies below illustrate this work; they do not yet establish a consistent molecular mechanism or a gene-expression basis for the B grade.

2005 · Medical Science Monitor

Recreational music-making modulates the human stress response: a preliminary individualized gene expression strategy

In 32 participants, recreational music-making reversed stress-associated gene-expression patterns in 19 of 45 measured genes, compared with six during quiet rest and none during continued stress. These included IL6 (interleukin-6), NR3C1 (the glucocorticoid receptor), NFKB1, IFNG and IL10.

Limit: A small study of blood gene expression. IL6 reversal also occurred in the resting group; the result concerns RNA expression, rather than a measured reduction in circulating IL-6 protein.

2025 · Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience

Music elicits different gene expression responses in the buccal cavity of age-related cognitive disorders patients and healthy controls

After music listening, LGALS3 expression decreased and CXCL8 increased in participants with age-related cognitive disorders. In healthy controls, THOP1 expression decreased. The study identified 31 differentially expressed genes in the patient group and seven in the controls.

Limit: Exploratory findings in peripheral tissue do not establish changes in brain tissue or therapeutic efficacy.

Research opportunities

Larger controlled trials can test whether these molecular findings replicate, how long they last, and how they relate to clinical outcomes. Measuring gene expression alongside symptoms, hormones and physiology can help distinguish the effects of music-making, music listening, isochronic stimulation and binaural beats.

OXTR and the wider candidate network provide targets for future investigation. Their involvement remains a research question.

Anxiety and PTSD are our starting points. We welcome researchers investigating other conditions, populations and applications.

How we assess the evidence

Universal Mind’s A–C grades summarise the evidence for a defined intervention and clinical focus. They consider systematic reviews, controlled trials, clinical guidance, consistency, study limitations and relevance to the intended use.

A
Supported by current clinical guidance and multiple controlled evidence syntheses.
B
Positive systematic evidence, with meaningful heterogeneity or a product translation gap.
C
Promising or indirect evidence. The proposed delivery itself is not yet established.

Grades describe the research basis, not a medical recommendation.

Each subchapter receives one trust score: three crosses for at least three double-blind randomized controlled trials; two for at least three single-blind randomized controlled trials; one for at least three peer-reviewed journal articles. The letter grade summarises the wider evidence and is not an average of those scores.

Mantra and mindfulness-based stress reduction

This assessment brings together research on mantra meditation, including Transcendental Meditation, and mindfulness-based stress reduction. Anxiety and PTSD are Universal Mind’s initial clinical focus.

Why B?

Positive systematic evidence, with meaningful heterogeneity or a product translation gap.

The B grade summarises the wider body of meditation research in this collection. Gene expression findings are examined separately below.

Key studies

  1. 2024 · Frontiers in Psychology / U.S. VA

    Randomised trials of mind-body interventions for PTSD

    Systematic review of RCTs

    Meditation- and mantra-based interventions had moderate strength of evidence versus passive controls in the PTSD trial record reviewed by VA-affiliated researchers.

    Limit: Comparator quality, heterogeneous measures and limited objective outcomes constrain interpretation.

  2. 2023 · Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing

    Mantram repetition and psychological distress

    Systematic review + meta-analysis

    Across the eligible comparison studies, mantram repetition produced a small pooled reduction in psychological distress.

    Limit: Only four of 12 retrieved studies measured distress with comparison groups.

  3. 2022 · International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health

    Mantra-based meditation and mental health

    Systematic review + meta-analysis

    RCT evidence showed small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety, depression, stress and post-traumatic stress for mantra-based meditation.

    Limit: The review found risk of bias and too few psychiatric samples or long-term follow-ups.

  4. 2018 · The Lancet Psychiatry / U.S. DoD funded

    Transcendental Meditation versus exposure therapy in veterans with PTSD

    Anchor randomised controlled trial

    In 203 veterans, Transcendental Meditation was non-inferior to prolonged exposure therapy for change in clinician-assessed PTSD symptoms at three months.

    Limit: One trial of a structured 12-session practice; it does not test passive or sub-audible mantra delivery.

Mindfulness and gene expression

Studies in this collection measure RNA expression, DNA methylation and inflammatory pathways alongside clinical outcomes. Findings vary by intervention, population, tissue and comparison group. Some trials report molecular changes; others find no difference.

Our research focus is to understand these relationships through larger, well-controlled studies, including work on OXTR and the wider candidate network. We welcome research across conditions, populations and mindfulness practices.

Clinical EMDR for PTSD

Clinician-delivered EMDR is an established trauma-focused psychotherapy for PTSD. Individual telehealth delivery is supported and increasingly used, while therapist-led online group G-TEP now has a growing evidence base that includes a 2026 meta-analysis.

Why A?

Supported by current clinical guidance and multiple controlled evidence syntheses.

The A grade applies to clinical EMDR for PTSD. Evidence for other conditions, self-help adaptations and molecular effects is examined separately.

Key studies

  1. 2023 · U.S. VA / Department of Defense

    VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of PTSD

    Clinical practice guideline

    The current U.S. military and veterans guideline places EMDR among the individual trauma-focused psychotherapies with the strongest recommendation for PTSD.

    Limit: A clinical guideline for qualified care; it does not validate an entertainment-platform adaptation.

  2. 2025 · Psychological Trauma / U.S. VA

    Telehealth versus in-person EMDR for veterans

    Multisite retrospective review

    Across 256 veterans, both delivery groups improved. Completion, reliable change and clinically significant change did not differ, although the in-person symptom reduction was larger.

    Limit: Treatment format was chosen rather than randomised. This supports individual telehealth delivery; it does not test online group EMDR.

  3. 2026 · Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy

    Effectiveness of the Group Traumatic Episode Protocol

    Systematic review + meta-analysis

    Twelve G-TEP studies produced a large pooled reduction in PTSD symptoms (Hedges' g=1.28), supporting a feasible group option particularly after collective trauma.

    Limit: The synthesis covers G-TEP generally, not online delivery alone or a veterans-only population, and the included methods and samples differed.

  4. 2023 · Frontiers in Psychology

    Video-conference Group EMDR G-TEP for frontline and emergency workers

    Randomised controlled trial

    Eighty-five participants completed a remotely delivered intensive group protocol; PTSD, anxiety and depression measures improved through six-month follow-up.

    Limit: The authors call the findings tentative, and no treatment effect was found for moral injury.

The VA’s 2025 research update reports 256 veterans for the telehealth comparison; the article abstract reports 279 eligible veterans. These published summaries use different denominators. VA update ↗ · Article ↗

EMDR and molecular research

Studies measure NR3C1, FKBP5 and SGK1 expression, microRNA, DNA methylation, cortisol and endocannabinoids. Several combine EMDR with trauma-focused CBT or other treatments. Their findings do not isolate an effect of eye movements or establish an OXTR effect.

Larger controlled studies can examine these relationships across treatments, ages and clinical outcomes.

Universal Mind’s research focus

The optional entertainment experience focuses on short-term stress reduction and felt connection. Audio and video rooms already support clinician-guided groups of up to nine people. Clinical programmes have separate assessment and care arrangements. The combined entertainment experience requires its own research; clinical EMDR evidence does not establish its effects.

What does a Jadad score mean?

Jadad is a five-point check of how a randomized trial reports assignment to groups, double blinding and people leaving the study. It is separate from the topic trust scores and the A–C research assessment.

0 / 5
No points awarded after assessment.
1 / 5
Limited reporting across the three checks; one point earned.
2 / 5
Some criteria are met; substantial reporting gaps remain.
3 / 5
Several criteria are met; two available points are missing.
4 / 5
Most criteria are met; one available point is missing.
5 / 5
All five points earned for randomization, double blinding and withdrawals.

Up to two points concern randomization, two concern double blinding and one concerns withdrawals. The same total can come from different combinations of criteria. The detailed assessment explains each point. A higher score does not establish that a treatment works or has no risk of bias. Participant blinding is often impractical in mindfulness and talking therapies.

Not yet assessed means no score has been assigned. Not applicable identifies a study outside this scale’s scope, such as a review or observational study.
