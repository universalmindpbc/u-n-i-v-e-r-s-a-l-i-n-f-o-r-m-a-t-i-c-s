Oxytocin pathway gene networks in the human brain

Quintana DS, Rokicki J, van der Meer D, Alnæs D, Kaufmann T, Córdova-Palomera A, Dieset I, Andreassen OA, Westlye LT. · Nature Communications · 2019 · Human post-mortem expression mapping and co-expression analysis

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

Human brain expression maps showed regional enrichment of oxytocin-pathway transcripts and co-expression with dopaminergic and muscarinic acetylcholine genes. The study provides anatomical context for an OXTR-centred research programme.

Attributed extracts from the research record

“Three oxytocin signaling genes have been frequently implicated in human social behavior: OXT (structural gene for oxytocin), OXTR (oxytocin receptor), and CD38 (oxytocin secretion).”
“Expression of the three selected oxytocin pathway genes was enriched in subcortical and olfactory regions and there was high co-expression with several dopaminergic and muscarinic acetylcholine genes, reflecting an anatomical basis for critical gene pathway interactions.”
“The oxytocin signaling system may interact with dopaminergic and muscarinic acetylcholine signaling to modulate cognitive state processes involved in complex human behaviors.”

Recorded limitations

Spatial co-expression is an association. This study did not test sound, vision or language interventions, Fibonacci-derived features, or causal interactions across the proposed target network.
