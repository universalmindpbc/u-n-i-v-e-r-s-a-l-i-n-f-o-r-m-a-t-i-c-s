Community singing intervention reduces maternal salivary cytokines and enhances maternal-infant cortisol synchrony in postnatal depression: biological findings from the SHAPER-PND trial

Kirkpatrick M, Priestley K, Bind RH, Rebecchini L, Borsini A, Estevao C, Hazelgrove K, Laijawala R, Alford S, Woods AJ, Crane N, Manoharan M, Burton A, Dye H, Osborn T, Greenwood L, Dazzan P, Fancourt D, Pariante C. · Brain, behavior, and immunity · 2026 · Biomarker analysis within a randomized trial

Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

After the first community-singing session, salivary IL-6 decreased by 26% in the singing group. The time-by-group interaction for IL-6 was significant. Later analyses also examined mother–infant cortisol synchrony and communication.

Recorded limitations

This measured salivary cytokine protein, not IL6 transcription. The biological subset was unbalanced, and speech correlations do not isolate the causal effect of particular words.
