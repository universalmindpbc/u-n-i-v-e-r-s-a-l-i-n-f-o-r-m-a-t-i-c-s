Irregular light schedules disrupt daily rhythms and dysregulate genes involved in neuroplasticity, motivation, and stress responses

Berbegal-Sáez P, Gallego-Landin I, Macía J, Valverde O. · Pharmacology, biochemistry, and behavior · 2025 · Controlled mouse light-schedule experiment

Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

Short-term changes to the light–dark schedule disrupted circadian rhythms and region-dependent expression of genes including Oxtr, Cry2 and Per2, despite limited behavioural changes.

Recorded limitations

Mouse light-schedule disruption is distinct from human screen use, EMDR or geometric stimulation. The abstract does not establish a single direction of Oxtr change across tissues and time points.
