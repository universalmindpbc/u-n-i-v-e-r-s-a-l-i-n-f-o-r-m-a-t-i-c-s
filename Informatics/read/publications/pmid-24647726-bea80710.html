Relative efficacy of mindfulness-based relapse prevention, standard relapse prevention, and treatment as usual for substance use disorders: a randomized clinical trial.

Bowen S, Witkiewitz K, Clifasefi SL, Grow J, Chawla N, Hsu SH, Carroll HA, Harrop E, Collins SE, Lustyk MK, Larimer ME. · JAMA psychiatry · 2014 · Randomized controlled trial

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

In 286 adults completing substance-use treatment, mindfulness-based relapse prevention reduced substance-use days and heavy drinking at 12 months relative to standard relapse prevention and usual aftercare.

Recorded limitations

Benefits varied by endpoint and time: standard relapse prevention delayed first drug use more effectively at six months. Results concern structured aftercare, not a stand-alone meditation cure for addiction.
