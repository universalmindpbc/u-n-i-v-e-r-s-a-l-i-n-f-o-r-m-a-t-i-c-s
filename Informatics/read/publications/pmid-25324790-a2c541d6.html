A healthy heart is not a metronome: an integrative review of the heart's anatomy and heart rate variability

Shaffer F, McCraty R, Zerr CL. · Frontiers in Psychology · 2014

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Attributed extracts from the research record

“Physiological coherence is used broadly and includes all of the specific approaches for quantifying the various types of coherence measures, such as cross-coherence (frequency entrainment between respiration, BP, and heart rhythms), or synchronization among systems (e.g., synchronization between various EEG rhythms and the cardiac cycle), auto-coherence (stability of a single waveform such as respiration or HRV patterns), and system resonance.”
“A coherent heart rhythm is defined as a relatively harmonic (sine-wave-like) signal with a very narrow, high-amplitude peak in the LF region of the HRV power spectrum with no major peaks in the VLF or HF regions. Coherence is assessed by identifying the maximum peak in the 0.04–0.26 Hz range of the HRV power spectrum, calculating the integral in a window 0.030 Hz wide, centered on the highest peak in that region, and then calculating the total power of the entire spectrum. The coherence ratio is formulated as: (Peak Power/[Total Power – Peak Power])”
“With practice, people can learn to breathe at their cardiovascular system's resonance frequency. This aligns the three oscillators (baroreflex, HR, and BP) at that frequency and moves the peak frequency from the HF range (≈0.2 Hz) to the LF range (≈0.1 Hz). Breathing at the resonance frequency more than doubles the energy in the LF band (0.04–0.15 Hz). This corresponds to the Institute of HeartMath's heart rhythm coherence, which is associated with a “narrow, high-amplitude, easily visualized peak” from 0.09 to 0.14 Hz”
“The resonance frequency model uses the mechanism of slow breathing at an individual’s resonance frequency, while the psychological coherence model adds and emphasizes the importance of the pattern and stability of vagal afferent traffic as important components of emotional self-regulation. Both approaches have been associated with increased overall HRV over time.”
“There is agreement that the concept of “coherence” in the heart rate signal is observed as a peak in the HRV power spectrum around 0.1 Hz, which reflects resonance frequency of breathing and resonance in the baroreflex. The authors’ “psychophysiological coherence model” advocates biofeedback training that actually creates a maximum of HRV variability at this virtual single frequency.”
“It is known that the power spectrum distribution of normal fluctuations in a healthy heart follows an 1/f pattern, characteristic of fractal signals. So, this leads us to juxtapose Coherence and simplicity vs fractals and complexity.”
