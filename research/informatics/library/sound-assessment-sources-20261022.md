## Approved wording restored, 22 October 2026

The five key-study findings and limitations now match the live Universal Mind portal at `/vogue/iam/science-research-data-v1.js` verbatim. The prior assessment shortened these without preserving the signed-off details. The browser check now compares all ten passages with the portal source to catch future drift. Article titles and canonical source URLs remain intact. Explanatory copy uses brain entrainment, isochronic stimulation and binaural beats.

- The 2018 528 Hz study reports oxytocin changing from 37.57 to 73.58 immediately after five minutes of music (about 1.96 times the initial mean). This is separate from the theta review’s 4–8 Hz band and Universal Mind’s 5–8 Hz research range. The portal's original cortisol numbers and nine-participant limitation are restored too. The publisher reports the cortisol result at 30 minutes using its response-lag adjustment.
- The 2005 music-making study reports 19/45 markers showing significant negative Pearson correlations across stress and recovery, versus 6/45 with rest and 0/45 with continued stress. Table 2 includes IL6 (r = −0.542, p = 0.030), NR3C1 (r = −0.499, p = 0.049), NFKB1, IFNG and IL10 in the music-making group. IL6 also shows reversal in the resting group. These results describe individual RNA-expression responses; they are not a demonstrated group-wide reduction in circulating IL-6 protein.
- The original published 2005 manuscript and Table 2 were readable through the [author manuscript copy on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8062381_Recreational_music-making_modulates_the_human_stress_response_A_preliminary_individualized_gene_expression_strategy); the publisher PDF endpoint served a browser challenge. No PDF download or rendered-table verification is claimed.
- The 2025 Frontiers paper reports LGALS3 downregulation and CXCL8 upregulation in the cognitive-disorder group, and THOP1 downregulation in controls. Results report 31 adjusted differentially expressed genes in patients and seven in controls. Those named results are now visible in the assessment.

Primary-source checks: the 2018 publisher results (sections 2.2 and 3.1), the 2005 original manuscript (Table 2 and discussion), the 2025 Frontiers abstract/results, and the 2025 anxiety review (participant count, reported effect classification and quality appraisal). Existing chapter quotations and collection data remain unchanged.
