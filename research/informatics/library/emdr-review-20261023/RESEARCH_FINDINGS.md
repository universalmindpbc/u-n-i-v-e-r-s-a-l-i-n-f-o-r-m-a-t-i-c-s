# Vision research: integration findings

23 October 2026

## Clinical and molecular coverage

The narrative contains 76 distinct references across four chapters and 15 topics. The full Vision search also retains 17 previously indexed light/ocular references, for 93 records, 50 identified journals and 3,040 recorded citations. The ten-paper molecular chapter contains three RNA papers, four DNA-methylation papers and three cortisol/endocannabinoid papers. Original Sound and Language editions remain unchanged.

The strongest directly relevant gene-expression addition measures NR3C1, FKBP5 and SGK1 in blood during trauma-focused psychotherapy. Its 21-person longitudinal sample combines EMDR and TF-CBT. FKBP5 and SGK1 fell at eight weeks; follow-up showed rebound. The 2021 microRNA publication uses the same underlying cohort. These two publications do not establish independent replication or an isolated eye-movement mechanism. [Primary paper](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.1891/EMDR-2023-0019).

Molecular records distinguish mRNA, microRNA, DNA methylation and circulating biomarkers. No verified OXTR-expression result was established by this targeted EMDR search. Several studies contain related clinical cohorts, and none validates the exact combined Universal Mind entertainment intervention.

## Children, adolescents and young adults

The children/adolescent topic has eight papers. Three verified assessor-blinded randomized comparisons meet the two-cross threshold. KIEM blinded assessors between EMDR and KIDNET but not between active treatment and waitlist; the record states that limitation. The 2021 hospital-related subthreshold-PTSD trial found no superior primary PTSD outcome over usual care. This neutral finding remains alongside positive clinical trials. [KIEM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324567/), [hospital-related PTSD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33709117/).

Five young-adult papers cover remote groups, students, supervised peer-delivered work, a brief early trial and intensive clinical treatment. Their age ranges are explicit: 13–24, 12–25 and 16–25 in three reports. Student status alone does not establish an exclusively 18–24 population. The intensive six-day programme combines prolonged exposure, EMDR and other activities; it is not evidence for a gentle entertainment session. [Remote group](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34512458/), [intensive programme](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39835632/).

These findings support further investigation with appropriately defined populations and outcomes. They do not establish a ready-to-deploy self-administered acute-trauma intervention for minors. The general product and clinician-led rooms are documented separately in PRODUCT_CONTEXT.md. No partnership or endorsement by UNICEF, Headspace or another organisation is claimed.

## EMDR-inspired and self-administered protocols — discussion only

Published self-administered programmes vary in guidance. STEP used a recorded session and worksheets, with screening and clinical support, in 34 mental-health clinicians. The child self-help trial used a parent-supported story-based programme during COVID-19. Neither is equivalent to unrestricted unguided trauma processing. The 2020 review found a very limited self-administered evidence base; its quoted improvement refers to an earlier combined internet-CBT/EMDR feasibility programme with clinician contact. These original papers remain in the approved self-help section. [STEP](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.1891/EMDR-D-20-00047), [children](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.1891/EMDR-D-20-00054), [review](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjo.2020.92).

Additional peer-reviewed component studies examine bilateral visual/tactile stimulation, moving sound and Flash. Their outcomes include memory distress, EEG and peripheral physiology. They do not establish a universal physiological “reset” or validate a particular app. These supplementary comparisons have not been added to the website in this pass. [Flash experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8732365/), [bilateral stimulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12641405/), [moving sound](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11290623/).
