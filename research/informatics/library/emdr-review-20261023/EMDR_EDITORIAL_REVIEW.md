The existing Vision library contains the four portal entries and three molecular studies involving EMDR. Integration means placing those entries into the narrative and assessment, without creating duplicates. Other existing Vision research on light and ocular biology remains available for later sections.

Ten additional candidates are recorded in `literature-update.json`. Each has a stable identifier, suggested placement, concise finding and limitation. This is a targeted update, not a completed systematic review.

| Candidate | Contribution |
| --- | --- |

| [Adult PTSD meta-analysis, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42773467/) | 65 studies and 3,342 participants, including follow-up outcomes. |

| [Online EMDR 2.0 group trial, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1452206/full) | A small randomised study after traffic accidents, with mixed outcomes and one-month follow-up. |

| [KIEM refugee-children trial, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324567/) | A 96-person randomised comparison of EMDR, KIDNET and waitlist in children aged 8–18. |

| [Eye movements versus retrieval alone, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39731040/) | A 91-person component trial finding no added neurocognitive benefit from eye movements on its measured outcomes. Include neutral findings alongside positive findings. |
| [Anxiety umbrella review, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880055/) | Eleven meta-analyses; most underlying comparisons involve PTSD, limiting generalisation to other anxiety disorders. |

These additions strengthen coverage of online treatment, groups, children, displaced populations and mechanisms. Study overlap between meta-analyses is not replication. A recent publication date does not establish methodological quality. Full methods checks, blinding classification and link reconciliation remain part of the implementation pass.

## Molecular research

Add a distinct **EMDR and molecular research** section using the three relevant papers already indexed:

- [2024 treatment-resistant depression methylation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38362742/): 30 participants receiving EMDR or trauma-focused CBT. Exploratory methylation findings include inflammation-related regions; methylation is distinct from direct gene-expression measurement.
- [2024 youth PTSD methylation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39060246/): EMDR or trauma-focused CBT; saliva DNA methylation and treatment-response associations. The treatment design does not isolate eye movements.
- [2026 microRNA study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42519422/): 24 participants across both therapies. The combined sample's miR-132-3p change survived multiple-testing correction; EMDR-only subgroup changes did not.

These studies belong in Vision and remain cross-searchable with Language where relevant. They support a molecular research section, but do not establish an OXTR effect or a molecular effect caused specifically by visual stimulation. Three publications also may contain related cohorts; check independence before describing replication.

## Bibliographic and scoring checks before formatting

1. **Protocol versus results:** the 2013 psychosis/PTSD protocol, PMID 23702050, needs pairing with the completed trial, PMID 25607833. Preserve the protocol and identify its role; do not treat planned outcomes as observed results.
2. **Study-design labels:** the 2014 Shapiro review (24626074), 2018 mechanisms review (30166975), 2014 Chen meta-analysis (25101684) and group review (33415797; DOI 10.1002/cpp.2549) need their actual publication types. Reviews do not become individual RCTs because they discuss trials.
3. **Quotation accuracy:** verify the phrase “Trauma As Usual” against the cited paper. TAU refers to treatment as usual. Fix a confirmed transcription error explicitly and retain the exact article wording elsewhere.
4. **Population placement:** cross-list general depression studies outside the veteran-specific subsection when their participants are not veterans. Keep the studies.
5. **Clinical-guidance trust scores:** the three guideline sections currently carry three crosses in the PDF. Under Ben's clarified rules, guidelines themselves do not meet a three-double-blind-RCT threshold. Present them as **Clinical guidance**; retain the separate A assessment. Confirm this display change with Ben alongside the headings.
6. **One topic score:** three crosses require at least three distinct double-blind RCTs; two require at least three distinct single-blind RCTs; one requires at least three peer-reviewed journal articles. A paper appearing twice does not count twice. Below the threshold, leave the topic ungraded. Display the single score alongside its paper count in the TOC and topic context, without individual-paper grades.
7. **Counts:** retain the PDF's stated 55 articles, 33 publications and 3,210 citations in the internal reconciliation. Audit all entries, repeated references and the meaning of “publications” before setting website totals; the current importer has 47 archived placements across 41 unique Vision records. This discrepancy does not establish omitted papers or a counting error by Ben. Resolve it entry by entry. Do not substitute 33 for a verified unique-paper count.
8. **Links:** validate each original destination, resolve moved pages through DOI/PubMed/publisher or official-agency pages, and record old and replacement URLs. A blocked automated request is not evidence that a link is dead. The LINKS presentation has a current DCJ destination: https://dcj.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/dcj/dcj-website/documents/about-us/facsiar/research-seminars/past-seminars/2022/Debbie-Haynesv2-.pdf .
9. **Citations:** retain existing recorded counts. New citation counts in the candidate file are Europe PMC counts with retrieval date and provider; do not silently mix them with older counts as if they were one live census.
10. **Jadad:** preserve the separate detailed-search filter and future scoring task. No guessed Jadad scores.
